The Story of America’s Birthday

July 4, 1776 — Philadelphia, Pennsylvania

A later painting by John Trumbull shows leaders bringing the Declaration to Congress.

A long time ago, before America was its own country, many families came across the ocean to make homes here. They built farms, towns, churches, shops, roads, and schools. They worked hard and helped their neighborhoods grow.

But the people who lived here did not get to make many big choices for themselves. A king far away in England made rules for them. Sometimes he made them pay money called taxes, but the people did not have someone nearby to speak for them and help make those rules.

The people thought, “That is not fair. Families should have a voice. People should be able to help choose their own leaders.”

So some brave leaders met together. They talked and talked. They did not all agree at first, because this was a very big decision. But they wanted the people in America to be free to make their own country.

A man named Thomas Jefferson helped write an important paper called the Declaration of Independence. It said that people are created with rights, and that the American colonies wanted to become a new country.

On July 4, 1776, the leaders agreed to the Declaration of Independence. That is why we call July 4th America’s birthday.

The new country was called the United States of America. The people still had hard work ahead, but they had begun something important. They wanted a country where people could have a voice, make laws together, and live with liberty.

So when we see flags, fireworks, and parades on Independence Day, we remember the beginning of America. We remember the people who wanted freedom, worked together, and helped start our country.

Tell it back: First, people made homes here. Then a king far away made unfair rules. Then leaders met and wrote the Declaration. On July 4th, America became its own country.
